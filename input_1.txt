GOAL:
A system to autonomously fill water from a large reservoir into a coffee machine's internal reservoir. The system should be able to operate by itself for an entire work day (10 hours) with absolutely minimal manual intervention. The only manual work needed should be to turn on the system at the beginning of the work day, refill the large reservoir at the end of the work day, and turn off the system at the end of the workday.
:: END GOAL

DESIGN:

COMPONENTS:
- large reservoir (10 gallons)
- coffee machine (note: already provided, immutable part of design) with internal reservoir
- low power, embedded computer (CONTROLLER)
- power adapter and plug (for computer)
- food grade tubing for water
- water pump
- power adapter & plug for water pump
- GPIO based cable connecting CONTROLLER to water pump's power supply
- transistor or relay that connects over GPIO to the CONTROLLER and water pump
- computer case
- waterproof tape, must be able to be submerged for an extremely long duration without deteriorating 
- water sensors
- GPIO based data transfer cables for water sensors
:: END COMPONENTS

CONNECTIONS:
- The computer is inside of the case.
- The ac power cable is plugged in and secured to the computer in the case.
- The computer & case is affixed with regular tape to the outside (non-water side) of the large reservoir.
- The water pump is affixed to the bottom of the inside of the large reservoir with waterproof tape (water side).
- The data transfer cable is connected to the pump and computer. Each side of the connection is wrapped in water proof tape. This 
- There are four water sensors. Two are dedicated to the large reservoir and two are dedicated to the coffee machine's internal reservoir. All are affixed with waterproof tape to inside of their respective reservoirs. 
- For each pair of water sensors, one is dedicated as the low sensor and the other is the high sensor. The low sensor detects when the reservoir is out of water. The high sensor detects when the reservoir is full. 
- For the large reservoir's water sensors, the low sensor is placed immediately above the top of the water pump. The high sensor is placed near the top: it should be at least 2-3 sensor length's from the absolute top of the reservoir to prevent spillage in case the large reservoir must be moved during operation.
- For the internal reservoir in the coffee machine, the low sensor is placed at the demarcated minimum fill line. The high sensor is placed at the demarcated max fill line. These demarcations are specified by the machine's manufacturer.
- The GPIO cables will connect to each water sensor and the CONTROLLER. These will be used to send the binary water/no water signals necessary.
- The GPIO cable from CONTROLLER to water pump's power supply will be used to turn on and off the pump.

::END CONNECTIONS

SYSTEM OPERATION:

The embedded computer aka the CONTROLLER will be connected to the sensors and pump via GPIO cables. The CONTROLLER will monitor water levels and activate the pump to move water from the large reservoir to the coffee machine's internal reservoir when appropriate.

There will be a switch installed in the pump's power supply. This switch will default to keeping the pump disconnected. Only a continuous signal from the CONTROLLER will be able to keep the pump energized. When the CONTROLLER is off, the pump will not be energized.

The CONTORLLER will keep a log of its actions. This append-only log will be structured. The log events will also have visibility levels of DEBUG, INFO, WARN, and FATAL. 

Any bug or error in the CONTROLLER's logic that causes it to crash will be caught by a top-level exception handling loop. This handler will ensure that the pump is turned off in all circumstances.  It will log this event as FATAL and move the system into state: UNRECOVERABLE ERROR - SYSTEM HALT. The log will include as much detail as possible for the exception. This state cannot be moved out of by the system. Manual intervention is required to recover.

Upon start up, the CONTROLLER will log at INFO level that it has started state: BOOTING.

In BOOTING, the CONTROLLER will check that its sensor and water pump cables are all connected and correctly working. If there are any problems encountered, the CONTROLLER will log a FATAL event describing which components are faulty. The CONTROLLER will move into state: SENSOR OR PUMP MALFUNCTION - SYSTEM HALT. This state cannot be moved out of by the system. Manual intervention is required to recover.

If the component checks succeed, the CONTORLLER will move the system to state: STARTING - RESET. This will be logged at INFO level. It will send a control signal to turn off the pump. The CONTROLLER will ensure that the pump is not running. If it cannot, then it will raise an exception (and follow that prescribed ensuing logic).

The sensor cables will be labeled by reservoir type and labeled as either low or high. These cables will send binary signals to the controller, indicating whether or not there is any water present at the sensor's location.

If the high sensor of the coffee machine's reservoir is triggered, then the CONTROLLER will deactivate the pump. [event, log INFO] [state: TARGET FULL, log INFO]

If the low sensor of the coffee machine's reservoir is triggered, then the CONTROLLER will activate the pump. [event, log INFO] [state: TARGET EMPTY, log INFO]

If the pump is active and the large reservoir's low sensor is triggered, the CONTROLLER will deactivate the water pump. [event, log INFO] [state: SOURCE EMPTY, log WARN]

Whenever the large reservoir's high sensor is active, the CONTROLLER will log an event of "full source" and will transition the system's state into full. This will be logged at the INFO level. This is an optional feature: if connected to an automated refilling source, this can be used as a signal to that system to stop filling the large reservoir.

For any WARN or FATAL level log event, the CONTROLLER will use email to send a notification to a human operator. This email will include the event description and the system's current state.

::END SYSTEM OPERATION

::END DESIGN
