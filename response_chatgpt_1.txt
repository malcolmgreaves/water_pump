FEASIBILITY:
The proposed system is feasible and well-structured. It includes necessary components such as water sensors, an embedded controller, a water pump, and structured logging with fail-safes. The design accounts for power control, waterproofing, and automatic error handling. The inclusion of both high and low sensors in both reservoirs ensures proper monitoring and operation. The use of a transistor or relay to control the pump and an append-only log with email alerts enhances reliability and maintainability.

ADJUSTMENTS:

Confirm Waterproofing for GPIO Cables: Ensure that GPIO data transfer cables and sensor connections are fully insulated and waterproofed. Consider using sealed connectors instead of waterproof tape for long-term reliability.

Add a Check Valve to Prevent Backflow: To avoid water siphoning or unintended flow from the coffee machine's reservoir, a one-way check valve should be placed in the tubing.

Power Failure Recovery: While system halt states prevent damage, consider adding a mechanism to detect and recover from unexpected power outages. The CONTROLLER should log an event and ensure the pump is off on restart.

Consider a Secondary Mechanical Fail-Safe: If the CONTROLLER fails or a sensor malfunctions, the system could overflow the coffee machine. A float valve inside the coffee machine’s reservoir would provide an additional physical safety measure.

FINAL SUGGESTIONS:

Expand Logging to a Remote Location: In addition to an append-only log, consider logging critical events to a cloud-based service for monitoring system health remotely.

Use a Non-Volatile Storage for Logs: If the controller restarts, logs should persist. Consider using a database or external storage instead of just an in-memory log.

Optimize Energy Efficiency: If the system runs continuously, power management strategies (e.g., sleep mode for the CONTROLLER when idle) could extend hardware lifespan.

Prototype and Test Failure Scenarios: Before deployment, simulate various failure cases (e.g., sensor disconnects, power failures, reservoir depletion) to validate the system’s resilience.
