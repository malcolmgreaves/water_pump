FEASIBILITY:
The current design is not fully feasible in its current form. While it includes a water reservoir, pump, sensors, and an embedded computer for automation, it lacks a clear mechanism to detect and control the water level inside the coffee machine’s internal reservoir. Without feedback from the coffee machine’s water level, the system risks overfilling or underfilling it.

ADJUSTMENTS:

Add a water sensor inside the coffee machine’s internal reservoir – This will allow the system to detect when the coffee machine needs more water and when to stop filling.
Modify the pump control logic – The embedded computer should use feedback from the coffee machine's reservoir sensor to activate or deactivate the pump.
Ensure proper water transfer tubing – A check valve should be added to prevent backflow, and the tubing should be securely connected to the coffee machine's reservoir inlet.
Improve waterproofing and mounting – Instead of regular tape, use industrial-grade mounting brackets or adhesives for securing components, especially the embedded computer.
Clarify the power source for the pump – If the pump is powered separately, ensure it has a proper relay or switch controlled by the embedded computer.

FINAL SUGGESTIONS:

Consider using a solenoid valve instead of a pump if the large reservoir is placed at a higher elevation than the coffee machine. This would allow gravity-fed water transfer and reduce power consumption.
Implement a software failsafe that stops the pump if the high sensor or internal reservoir sensor fails.
If possible, integrate a wireless alert system to notify when the large reservoir needs refilling.
Use food-grade tubing to ensure safe water transfer to the coffee machine.
With these modifications, the system will be fully autonomous and reliable for an entire workday.
